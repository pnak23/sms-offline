# Changelog

Khmer Primary School Management System (KPSMS). Versioning rules: `docs/RELEASE.md`. The database schema number (the last migration) is separate from the program version and is listed per release.

## 1.0.0 — Stable — 2026-11-01 (database schema 10)
This is the first version that carries the system's name and a version number. Everything built in Phases 0–5 is in it. **It has not been installed or tried on any computer other than the development PC**, and nothing in it has been accepted by the school (see `docs/FINAL-SCHOOL-ACCEPTANCE.md`).

### Added
* **System identity and version management.** One authoritative file (`release/release.json`) gives the name, short name (KPSMS), developer (PHEA NAK), version, channel and copyright; the sign-in screen, the sidebar, Settings → About, the window titles, both logs, backup manifests, the diagnostics export, the installer name and the Windows file properties are all derived from it. `node scripts/version.mjs check | sync | set` keeps `package.json` ×3, `package-lock.json` and `backend/pom.xml` in step and the full build checks it. New public endpoint `GET /api/v1/system/about`; `GET /api/v1/system/info` now also reports the technology versions and the schema number.
* **Readiness and sign-off.** Settings → About lists what is still pending before the school accepts the system, in five distinct states (development completed · configuration pending · official information pending · physical testing pending · final school acceptance completed). Only the administrator can record the last one (migration V6, audited, can be withdrawn); a build or a test cannot. Screens that depend on information the school has not confirmed show a "not official yet" notice until then, and printed report cards carry a small "draft rules" line until the grading rules are signed off.
* **Flexible attendance sessions** (migration V7). The administrator keeps the sessions of the school day per academic year (whole day, morning, afternoon or any name; optional start and end times; on/off; order; "confirmed by the school"). Nothing is hard-coded and no time is invented. A sheet keeps a snapshot of its session's name and times, so editing the schedule never rewrites history. The database refuses a second record for the same student, day and session. The attendance screen offers the sessions as buttons (hidden when there is only one), ticks the ones already taken, remembers the last one used and can start a session from another one taken the same day. Settings → Attendance sessions can also copy a year's sessions into another year.
* **Configurable report card.** Settings → Report card: logo, national header, student photo (off by default), attendance, rank, comment, 0–3 signature boxes with captions, a closing note, subject order; a live preview uses the real card component. The defaults are the card as it was. A card that would not fit one A4 page (for example the national header, a photo, three signature boxes, a note and twelve subjects, which was 1214 px against the 1122 px a page holds) measures itself and is set in a slightly tighter type instead of spilling onto a second page; the settings page says when that happens.
* Fee currency was already configurable (riel, dollars or both); the readiness list keeps it "configuration pending" until the school chooses.
* **Roll of honour (តារាងកិត្តិយស)** (added 2026-11-04, before any release). A **page of its own** (sidebar entry, and a button on the Report cards page): the top 5 or top 10 students of a class for the chosen examination, printed as a certificate on one A4 page, with ten artwork styles to choose from (classic gold filigree, lotus, Angkor towers, stars and ribbon, royal red and gold, laurel, blue guilloche, kids' rainbow, simple line that saves ink, rising sun). The names are the report cards' own ranks, so the two always agree; students tied on the last rank are all listed (the page says so) and a student with an incomplete result is never listed. Optional office line, place and a lunar date typed by the school (the program does not compute one); "every class of the year" prints one page per class. The national header (ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រកម្ពុជា / ជាតិ សាសនា ព្រះមហាក្សត្រ) and a flourish under it are **always** at the top, in every style, whatever the "national header" switch of Settings → Printed reports says (its words still come from those settings). The artwork is drawn by code, so nothing is downloaded and no image is bundled. **One saved default** ("Save as the default" on that page, which also shows a live preview of the real sheet): the number of names, the artwork and the **five texts of the school's own sample certificate** are saved once, so printing only means choosing the date. The five texts are the places the school marked: the block at the top left (the cluster / office line, the school, the class), the line under the title, the date lines at the bottom right (the lunar date, the place, the date), and the two signature titles ("បានឃើញ និងឯកភាព / នាយកសាលា" and "គ្រូបន្ទុកថ្នាក់"). **Every word of them can be changed**; nothing is fixed in the program. Words in braces are filled in for each page: {school}, {class} (right on every page of "every class"), {exam}, {date} (the date picked when printing, in its Khmer long form), {month} and {year} of that date; clicking a word puts it where the cursor is. So choosing another date changes the month, the year and the date lines by itself. Each box has a **font toolbar** like a small text editor's (font, size, bold, italic, underline, alignment, and "keep each line on one line", which shrinks a long line instead of wrapping it), and the date column is wider so a long lunar date stays on one line. The spacing, sizes and colours of the title block, the date lines and the signatures were matched to the school's own sample by measurement (a gold title, black text, the signature titles bold, room to sign). **Back** from the preview returns to the roll of honour page with everything that was chosen, not to the list of students. The boxes keep **spaces and line breaks** exactly as typed (up to 4 lines and 300 characters each) and print that way. The default is stored with the program's other settings (new `honor.*` keys, no database migration), never in the browser.
* **Roll of honour: ten more artwork styles** (added 2026-11-06, 20 in all; classic gold stays the default). After the first ten come: emerald and gold flames (ក្បាច់ខ្មែរបៃតង-មាស), rice ears (កួរស្រូវ), sugar palm (ដើមត្នោត), rose (ផ្កាកុលាប), crescent moon and stars (ព្រះច័ន្ទ និងផ្កាយ), book and pencil (សៀវភៅ និងខ្មៅដៃ), national blue and red bands (ពណ៌ទង់ជាតិ), old paper in thin sepia lines (ក្រដាសបុរាណ) and modern geometric in thin lines (ទំនើបធរណីមាត្រ), the last two drawn to use little ink, and sea waves (រលកសមុទ្រ). All are drawn by code (no image file, nothing downloaded); the first ten are drawn exactly as before (a test keeps a fingerprint of each); a saved default of any style keeps working (no migration). The ten Khmer names are the developer's own and have not been read by a native speaker. The patterns are stylised, not authentic Khmer ornament, and nothing was printed on paper.
* **The installer no longer leaves a copy of itself on the PC** (source only, added 2026-11-06; it takes effect in the next installer build, the existing one is stale and unchanged). electron-builder's installer copies itself to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\kpsms-updater\installer.exe` (226 MB) and its uninstaller never removed it. The new `desktop/build/installer.nsh` removes that one file after the install and when the program is uninstalled, and its folder only if empty: it never deletes anything else, never recurses, never touches the school's data, and the build stops if the file name it relies on is not what it expects. Tested with a static guard (17 unsafe variants refused), with a tiny installer built from the real file (a dummy file in that folder survives), and with scripts for the real installers (D22 and an upgrade test) that are not yet run.
* **Monthly ranking list (ភាពចំណាត់ថ្នាក់ប្រចាំខែ)** (added 2026-11-05, before any release). A **page of its own** (sidebar entry, and a button on the Report cards page) that prints EVERY student of a class for one examination, in rank order, with the total score, the average (the percentage divided by 10), the rank and the grade; girls and boys are marked (ស / ប), each row takes the colour of its grade (A green to F red), and a student whose result is incomplete comes last with no rank and the remark "មិនទាន់គ្រប់ពិន្ទុ" (nobody is left out). Under the table: how many students there are, how many passed and how many did not (each with the girls), the date and the two signature titles. The figures are the report cards' own, and the grade names and the pass mark are the grading scale's in Settings (the owner's picture was only an example, and the default scale is still a placeholder). The national header is always printed on top. It has one look (the school's sample), but every word is the school's: six text boxes with words in braces (the roll of honour's six plus the school year and the six figures), each with its own font toolbar, and the ten column headings, all saved as ONE default (`ranking.*` settings, no migration), so printing usually means choosing only the date. A class too big for a page continues on the next one with the table heading repeated; "every class" prints one list per class; a preview beside the form shows the real students of the chosen class; Back from the print page returns to the form with everything chosen. The table can be coloured (heading, rows by grade) or transparent (only the lines are printed), and that choice is part of the saved default too. The text-box component of the roll of honour became generic to serve both pages.
* **Subjects with sub-subjects (migration V8)** (added 2026-11-05, before any release; the owner approved the design of `docs/PHASE0-ARCHITECTURE.md` §16). A subject is a **main subject** or a **sub-subject** of one (two levels): Settings → Academic lists them indented and adds a sub-subject under its main subject (it keeps that main subject; a wrongly placed one is deactivated and created again), and the grade table lets a grade study a sub-subject only if it studies its main subject (switching a main subject off for a grade takes its sub-subjects with it). An examination now keeps **its own copy** of its subjects: the names and order it was set up with and, for a main subject made of sub-subjects, one row per sub-subject; scores are typed at the lowest level (sub-subjects are listed under their main subject in the score entry), the main subject is the **sum** of its sub-subjects (its maximum too, read-only), and the totals, average, percentage and rank count **main subjects only** (never a sub-subject on top of its main subject). From the first stored score the structure is frozen (nothing added or removed; a maximum or weight can still be corrected), and renaming, switching off, re-ordering or narrowing subjects afterwards changes nothing in an existing examination (results, report cards and rows are byte for byte the same). The report card lists main subjects; Settings → Report card has a switch (off by default) that adds the sub-subjects under them. Homework and the teachers of a class stay at the main-subject level. V8 is additive and changes nothing already stored: every old examination keeps its names and scores and is marked frozen if it has scores. **Not built:** the quiz components (how quizzes count towards a monthly or final score), which wait for the school's real score record; nothing about them is assumed.
* **Removing a subject nothing uses, and a Grade 3 demo subject configuration for the sample school** (added 2026-11-05, before any release). Settings → Academic can now remove a subject or sub-subject that no examination, class teacher assignment or homework refers to (after a confirmation; a main subject only after its sub-subjects; one that is used is switched off instead): `DELETE /api/v1/subjects/{id}`, with `SUBJECT_IN_USE` and `SUBJECT_HAS_SUB_SUBJECTS`. The fictional sample school (`npm run sample:seed`) now builds a **demo** Grade 3 configuration from `scripts/sample-data/grade3-demo-subjects.json`: 7 main subjects and 17 sub-subjects with sample marks, every name labelled "(គំរូ)" in Khmer and "[DEMO]" in English, no quiz, nothing in the program's logic, Grade 3 only (the other grades keep their own list), scored at sub-subject level and **checked before the script finishes**. `--dir <folder>` builds the sample school somewhere else. It is demo data, not the school's curriculum, and its Khmer names are an unreviewed AI translation.
* **Weekly timetable (កាលវិភាគ) (migration V10)** (added 2026-11-06, before any release; the owner approved the plan in `docs/TIMETABLE-PLAN.md` and added requirements). A class's week, a teacher's week (with the total number of lessons) and a student's week (on the student's page), with **days and time slots that the school enters itself: the program ships none and invents none** (a year starts with no slot and the default Monday–Friday week, which nobody saved; Settings → Timetable says so until the administrator signs the readiness item off). A lesson is a main subject **and** a teacher, never one without the other; the class's own teacher is the default; a teacher cannot be in two classes at once and the refusal names the other class; a break is a shaded row with no lesson; a class with no lessons, a year with no slots and a student with no class are normal, empty states. Copying a class's week, or a whole year's, is **previewed first** (which lessons are copied and which are skipped, with the reason of each), and a copy only reads its source: a closed year can be copied from and is never changed. Every change is in the history of changes with the user and the time. A4 portrait printing by default, landscape on request, and an Excel file, through the same report mechanism as every other report. Entirely offline; the grid has its own scrolling frame, so a week with Saturday and Sunday on a small screen never scrolls the page. The Khmer wording is my own, confirmed acceptable by the owner on 2026-11-06 (`frontend/i18n/REVIEW.md` §19).
* **A premium dashboard: a hero and six read-only charts** (added 2026-11-06, before any release; the owner approved a 20-item list). The dashboard keeps every figure it had (people, today's attendance, the backup state, the current year, the latest examinations, events, fees still due) and now opens with a deep-blue banner with gold accents (the year, today's date, a greeting, and three quick links: take attendance, add a student, examinations) and icon tiles. Below them, six charts drawn by hand as SVG (**no chart library, nothing downloaded**), loaded **separately** so the figures appear at once and the charts follow: **students by grade** (boys and girls), **gender** (a student whose gender is not recorded stays in the totals as a third part), **attendance over the last 14 recorded school days** (a day nobody recorded is a **gap** in the line, drawn as a hatched band, never as zero; it counts by the school's chosen method, days or sessions), **class fill** against each class's stored capacity (a class with no capacity stored shows its head count alone: no capacity is ever made up), **the latest examination** (the average of each class and the pass / fail / not-complete split, taken from the same calculation as the report cards, with the "not the school's official rules yet" notice while the grading rules are unsigned) and, only when fees are switched on, **fees collected over the last six months, one block per currency, never added together or converted**. Every chart has a written summary, a "show as table" view, one keyboard tab stop (arrow keys, Home and End move between bars or days and the sentence of the chosen one is read out), touch-friendly details, and an empty state that says what is missing instead of drawing nothing. Animations are off for a person who asked their system for less motion and on the Low-memory preset. The new endpoint `GET /api/v1/dashboard/charts` only reads (a test compares the row count of every table before and after); no migration and no stored data changed. **Not part of this release, by the owner's decision:** a "today's lessons" panel (the timetable stays independent of the dashboard). The Khmer wording is my own, confirmed acceptable by the owner on 2026-11-06 (`frontend/i18n/REVIEW.md` §20).

### Changed
* **The dashboard's backup status is a quiet line while all is well** (changed 2026-11-06, before any release; the owner found a large green box that never changes to be noise). When a good backup is less than 24 hours old, the dashboard shows one line (a small check mark, "Data backup", the message, a link to the Backup page) with no coloured box, and an **information button** that opens the complete note: a backup kept only on the laptop does not protect against a failed SSD, theft or loss of the laptop, or physical damage that destroys it (closed by default; it can be opened with the mouse, the keyboard or a finger). When there is no good backup, or the newest one is 24 hours old or more, the yellow warning box is **exactly as before**: its note always visible and the "back up now" button. The Backup page and the hand-over guide still state the limits in full. The backup logic, the 24-hour rule, retention, triggers and storage locations are unchanged. One new Khmer text (the button's label, my own, `frontend/i18n/REVIEW.md` §20, confirmed acceptable by the owner on 2026-11-06 with the rest of that section).
* **Notifications are no longer printed.** A notification on the screen (for example "saved as PDF", which appears beside the Save button) could be printed on top of the document, because the print rules hid the lock and backend overlays but not the notification area. It is hidden in print media now, for every printed page. Found by looking at the real PDF of the ranking list, not by a check.
* **Settings → Academic: a cleaner subject list.** The subjects were a four-column grid in which a sub-subject landed beside unrelated subjects and every row carried three text links. They are now **one list**: each main subject is a row of its own with its sub-subjects grouped and indented under it, a small count ("4 sub-subjects"), a "+ sub-subject" button and two small icons (rename, remove); a subject that is switched off is dimmed; a short legend explains the switch, the sub-subjects and why a used subject cannot be removed. **Adding and renaming open in a pop-up** (it used to open at the end of the list), the add-subject button is beside the heading, and the grade table shows main subjects in bold with their sub-subjects below. No behaviour or data changed.
* **Pagination on the long lists of Settings → Academic.** The subject list and the grade table are now shown a page at a time (5 main subjects to a page by default; 5, 10, 20 or all), always by **main subject**, so a main subject and its sub-subjects stay together. Under each list: "showing 1–5 of 15 main subjects", first / previous / numbered / next / last buttons, and a "per page" choice that is kept (in memory only, nothing in the browser) while the program is open. After adding or renaming a subject the list goes to the page that holds it. The pager is a reusable component (`ListPager`) that draws nothing while a list is short. The students, teachers and classes lists are paged the same way (10 to a page by default; 10, 20, 50, 100 or all): the whole list is still loaded, so the Excel file has every student; a new search or filter starts at page 1; a teacher or class just added is shown on its own page. Other lists (the students of one class, promotion, homework, fees, attendance, history, reports) still show everything.
* **Homework marks: homework is now an independent module (migration V9).** A homework can be about a main subject or a sub-subject, and can have a maximum mark ("has a score"; without it it is a record only, as before). For every assignment the teacher records, student by student, a submission status (submitted, late, not submitted, excused), a mark when there is a maximum, and a remark; the list shows who handed it in and the class average. A student's profile has a Homework history; the Reports page has three homework reports (the homework of a class, the marks of one homework, the summary of the students), each saying it is not part of the examination. **Homework marks are never part of examination scores, totals, averages, rankings or report cards** (the school's rule; a test proves it). A student who did not hand something in is never counted as 0. Database schema 9.
* **A preferred fee currency.** Settings → Fees has a "preferred currency" (riel until the school chooses dollars) that shows when both currencies are allowed: a new fee category starts in it and lists and the fee report show it first. It changes no recorded money; nothing is converted or combined.
* **Local backups on the laptop are now the primary backup method.** The dashboard always shows the state of the backups on this laptop (green when a good backup is less than 24 hours old; a warning, with a "back up now" button, when there is none or the newest good one is 24 hours old or more), and so does the top of the Backup & Restore page. Automatic backups are unchanged (when the program closes normally, before an upgrade, before a restore); there is still no timer, no automatic USB copy and no cloud. The reminder to copy a backup to a USB stick is now **optional and off by default** (it was a reminder after 7 days). The screens and the hand-over guide now say plainly that a backup kept on the laptop does not protect against a failed SSD, theft or loss of the laptop, or physical damage.
* **The application icon is now the KPSMS logo** supplied by the owner (blue rounded square, a Khmer-style roof, two pupils and an open book). It is the icon of the program, the installer, the shortcuts and the window. The placeholder icon is gone; the owner's original file is kept as `desktop/build/logo-source.png` and the icon files are made from it by `desktop/scripts/make-icon.mjs`.
* **The window now suits a 14-inch Full HD laptop (and smaller logical workspaces).** The window follows the screen: never bigger than the usable area, maximised on a laptop-sized screen, and it can be made as small as 880×440 (it used to be 1200×650, which made every page scroll sideways at 125 % or 150 % Windows scaling on a 1366×768 laptop). A narrow window collapses the side menu to its icons by itself and opens it again when the window widens; a person's own choice wins.
* **A one-page Khmer hand-over guide** (`docs/HANDOVER-KM.html`): opening and signing in, attendance, printing a report card, backups, the recovery key. A draft until a Khmer speaker has read it.
* **The screen is used in full.** Pages no longer stop at 1100 px and float in the middle of a wide or maximised window; form pages keep a readable 960 px width from the left.
* Existing attendance is migrated without loss: each academic year gets a "whole day" session (and "morning" / "afternoon" where old sheets used them); sheet and record ids are kept. If old data had the same student twice in one session and day (a student who changed class and was marked in both), the older record is left out and written to the audit trail.
* **Attendance is counted by days as well as by sessions** (added 2026-11-04, before any release). A day counts once per student however many sessions it had: present in the morning and absent in the afternoon is one **partial** day, never two days. Daily statuses are Present, Late, Partial, Absent and Leave, worked out from the records and never stored, so changing, renaming or switching off a session cannot change the past. The school's **official method** is a setting (Settings → Attendance sessions; days until the school chooses); the dashboard and the report card follow it, and the history, the reports and the Excel files offer both. Every figure names its unit (ថ្ងៃ days, វគ្គ sessions). With one session a day both methods give the same numbers, but the default tables now have a Partial column. New readiness item: the school's choice of counting method.
